What the app does—and what it cannot do.

Ticket recognition

LottoLens PH uses browser-side optical character recognition (OCR) to turn visible ticket text into candidate numbers. OCR is a convenience feature, not official ticket validation. The interface therefore requires users to review editable numbers before checking.

Result comparison

For supported 6-number games, a play line is treated as a set of six distinct numbers. The checker highlights the intersection between that set and the six published winning numbers. Number order is ignored.

History-inspired suggestions

The “history-inspired” generator begins with cryptographically generated browser randomness and applies only a small weight to historical frequency. This is descriptive, not predictive. A fair lottery draw is independent: a number being “hot” or “cold” in the stored sample does not change its probability in the next draw.

Balanced and pure-random modes

Pure random samples six numbers uniformly without replacement. Balanced random still starts uniformly, then accepts combinations with a moderate odd/even and low/high split and few consecutive pairs. That makes the output look less extreme, but it does not make it more likely to win.

Data quality

The app stores a rolling 12-month history for its five supported six-number games. Backfilled draws retain their publisher and source link; publisher reports are not official ticket validation. Daily updates append published results and remove draws older than 12 months. Archive coverage may contain gaps when a source is unavailable. Older draw dates can be fetched on demand, with progress shown during lookup. These older results stay in the current session, are not added to stored history, and do not enter number suggestions. Only the game and draw date are sent for archive lookup; ticket photos remain in your browser. If a result is delayed, stale, or inconsistent, official PCSO information controls.
